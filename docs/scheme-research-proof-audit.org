* Audit decision

At this audit's baseline, the [[file:research/scheme-dsl/index.org][DSL source ledger]] contained eight papers:
A22, F16, H13, F20, S20 and L25 concerned the language/kernel; H18 and
B22 added deletion and incremental-maintenance constraints. Later B23, X25,
FL25 and S24 readings add Provider, physical-plan and nested-fact
questions without extending the audited proof coverage. These are
distinct from the separate
[[file:research/agent-inference/index.org][agent-inference collection]]. The next step is to connect paper assumptions
to admitted Scheme IR and observable transactions. Adding syntax, quoting a
paper or passing an abstract proof is insufficient for design closure.

The current checked subset has a coherent division: Scheme constructs program
values; admission exposes domains, lexical captures and dependencies; the native
core owns evaluation; retained Sessions own source/result publication; bounded
evidence binds a specific program and snapshot. No paper establishes the safety
of arbitrary host closures. Therefore unrestricted callback admission, silent
domain clamping and hidden dependencies are excluded from the checked subset.
Trusted host construction remains outside the proof boundary.

Audit baseline: GitHub PR #1 head =bb4f58a826ac37e85974c66ce504ba1a7c7b57da=.
The 13-Suite / 138-Case receipt binds native source =0e1141e=; its later
documentation child does not expand that semantic scope. Historical negative
performance results and the failed three-arm measurement remain failures or
negative evidence. They do not become benefit claims through this proof audit.

* Current proof ownership

Lean and Quint have disjoint acceptance claims. Lean owns universally quantified
semantic and algebraic laws; Quint owns reachable protocol states, interleavings,
cancellation, draining and publication. Native execution remains a third
independent implementation discriminator, not a theorem from either collection.

| Concern | Lean claim | Quint claim |
|---------+------------+------------|
| Language and evaluation | Typing, lexical lowering, monotonicity, least fixed points and finite-height laws | No duplicate language/fixed-point calculus |
| Source updates | Dependency/certificate frames and exact source-count arithmetic in SourceUpdateFrame | Session transaction, provisional/bounded work, failure and publication traces |
| Actors | Checkpoint-distance arithmetic; no mailbox or task state machine | Work-key admission, stale/duplicate replies, ready dispatch, credits, cancel/drain/close |
| Indexes and caches | Immutable identity lookup and ordered bucket extension equations | Private staged installation, version/root alignment and visible-state isolation |
| Providers / SCCs | Abstract frontier, closure and quotient laws | Concrete finite injection/consume/merge/remap/refusal sequences; semantic oracles are checks on those sequences, not extra universal algebra proofs |
| Evidence | Certificate/closed-model absence validity and dependency reuse | Receipt generation/source binding across changes and provisional/bounded status |

The Lean SessionPublication and NativeSessionPublication transition replicas,
RoundAdmission protocol state and ReadyComponents task-completion state have
been removed. Source-count laws remain in SourceUpdateFrame. The publication
wrapper theorem in SourceCertificates was removed; typed patch/certificate
laws remain. Quint models and their negative controls retain protocol ownership.
Historical receipts still certify their original sources, not the current set.
Removing those Lean replicas retires their universal protocol-trace claims;
finite TLC exploration does not inherit them. A future unbounded temporal
claim needs a Quint proof/refinement, not another Lean state-machine copy.

Generation has no maximum in either protocol's =Init/Next/Spec=. It ranges over
=Nat= and can keep increasing. =ExplorationDepth= and =ExplorationBound= exist
only for TLC state-space exploration; configuration values do not limit runtime
or constitute an all-generations theorem. Repeated source cuts are allowed,
so a stale job cannot become current merely because source bytes return to an
earlier value (ABA). The removed Lean publication replicas no longer supply
unbounded generation/trace claims. Current finite Quint checks neither inherit
those claims nor establish Gerbil trace refinement.

Safety is not liveness. Neither Quint model claims a solver eventually finishes,
or a transaction eventually wins against continuous competing publication.
A fairness/termination claim needs an explicit scheduling assumption and a
separate proof. Abort preservation is encoded as a required action contract;
model checking does not establish that native exceptions implement it.

Previously GitHub's =formal= job ran Lean only. It now executes the common
[[file:../justfile][Justfile proof gate]], including the Quint models and intentional faults. TLC is
pinned to the official 1.7.4 jar and verified by SHA256. The gate accepts
=ASCENT_FORMAL_GENERATION_CUTOFF= independently of protocol semantics; GitHub
uses 4, the default exploration configuration uses 2. Counterexample checks
require both TLC's violation exit code and the expected violated property;
parse/tool crashes cannot count as successful fault detection.

* Absence certificate construction budget (2026-10-09)

The [[https://thomas.gilray.org/pdf/seamless-deductive.pdf][A22 paper]], §2.1, defines positive evaluation by the least fixed point of
the immediate-consequence operator. Our absence verifier uses an independently
checked finite inductive invariant: it contains the supplied seeds, is closed
under every admitted rule instance, and excludes the ground target. Exhausting
a work or material budget establishes none of these conditions.

The [[https://kmicinski.com/assets/byods.pdf][B23 paper]], §3 and §5.1, separates relation concretization from its physical
representation; an equivalence class can represent quadratically many tuples.
This motivates auditing downstream consumers as well as the Provider. It does
not establish that our explicit proof-node or certificate representation is
compressed. These conclusions are our design analysis, not paper claims about
Gerbil certificates.

=candidate/provenance.ss= now reserves each distinct source or pending derived
row before copying an absence proof node. The existing certificate limits count
both kinds together: 4096 rows, 32768 cells and 1024 cells per row. The producer
also rejects a combined source/candidate schema over 64 relations before replay.
On exhaustion, it publishes =bounded= with no nodes or closure. The final
certificate shape check and independent rule-instance verifier remain active.
Ordinary positive witnesses keep their admitted derived-fact budget.

The native =std/test= source qualification includes exact row/cell boundaries,
both overruns, duplicate seeds/clauses, exact/overflowing row arities,
a 65-relation combined schema and a 5120-node ordinary positive
witness. Exact-boundary certificates independently verify as =valid=. Restoring
the previous producer through an isolated load path makes the new node-status
assertion fail. This is a discriminator for early reservation, not a universal
heap bound: indexes, terms, input references and temporary joins have separate
costs. Large compressed Provider results still require a separately designed
compressed invariant certificate; they are not admitted by raising these limits.

* Shared material reservation: Scheme, Lean and Quint (2026-10-09)

Finite stratified replay had the same late-size-check gap as absence proof
construction. =candidate/certificate-limits.ss= now owns a private invocation
row/cell budget. Both =candidate/provenance.ss= (absence only) and
=candidate/finite-evidence.ss= reserve each distinct row before copying it into
their private index. Source and derived rows share the quota; duplicate
membership is checked first. Finite replay rejects an oversized combined schema

then enters its terminal =bounded= path and returns no partial material.

* Frozen negative probes: witness stopping and charged absence (2026-10-09)

Re-reading [[https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~olhotak/pubs/oopsla20c.pdf][F20]], §4.7, confirms the lower-stratum premise: negative
dependencies refer strictly to earlier completed strata. Our transfer is to
probe a frozen relation for one matching binding. A matching row rejects the
negative clause immediately; proving absence still requires all rows. This is
our implementation decision, not a cost theorem from the paper. A22's positive
immediate-consequence semantics (§2.1) does not itself justify negation.

=candidate/finite-evidence.ss= previously kept scanning after a witness, which
could turn an already decided negative clause into =bounded=. It now stops at
the first witness or budget refusal. The existing final absence-continuation
step remains charged, and refusal never supplies an absence continuation.
The stratum scheduler, binding function and independent verifier are unchanged.

[[file:../packages/proofs/lean/StratifiedNegation.lean][StratifiedNegation]] adds seven laws for the budgeted probe: completed
truth equals =List.any=, remaining fuel decreases, absence costs exactly the
row count plus one, a first witness ignores any suffix, row count plus one is
sufficient, binding truth agrees with =matchingRow=, and completed probes
preserve the existing negative-clause environment semantics. Eight compile-time
examples discriminate first/last witnesses, refusal and the extra absence step.
Rows and binding outcomes are supplied; frozen relation enumeration and native
binding correctness remain explicit representation obligations.

[[file:../packages/proofs/quint/NegativeProbe.qnt][NegativeProbe]] owns the finite probe/refusal protocol. It checks witness
and absence soundness, terminal failure and position/fuel bounds. Intentional
early-absence, refusal-as-absence and resume-after-refusal variants must violate
their designated invariants. The finite input lists and budgets are exploration
controls, not general liveness or source-publication claims.
The correct model has 40 reachable states; all three fault controls produce
the expected invariant counterexample.

Native controls use a 1024-row relation. A first-row witness completes with
one charged probe; a last-row witness needs 1024. Absence needs 1025 per
evaluation and 2050 across the two actual fixed-point passes; 2049 refuses.
An empty relation similarly needs two charged continuations across both passes.
Every completed certificate is independently verified at the same budget.
These exact operation controls establish removed probes, not a wall-clock or
whole-engine speedup. No semantic, material or work limit is increased.
