* Design consequence
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: datafun-s20-consequence
:END:

The 2016 paper's later-work statement about higher-order optimization is
historical.  This 2020 paper supplies a semantic-preserving route for a
typed higher-order language, including a notion of change for a
function.  It is evidence that a long-term Gerbil language could have
higher-order operators *inside* fixed-point computations without
permanently giving up semi-naive evaluation.  It is not evidence that
the current planner already handles an opaque Scheme procedure.

A conservative first stage keeps functions higher-order at program
construction time and lowers admitted programs to first-order named
relations and visible positive atoms. That finite stage now has a
structural insertion-change interpreter and a [[file:e4-positive-change.org][local E4 receipt]]. It
does not supply Datafun's typed monotonicity proof or general function
changes. The result type and optimization theorem in the paper have
their own scope; they do not validate an arbitrary host-language FFI
boundary.

The paper's change structures here describe increasing changes.
Removing or replacing source facts is not covered by that argument.
The current retained Gerbil session can recompute from the accepted
source snapshot; any future deletion-aware higher-order algorithm
requires a separate semantic equivalence and rollback receipt.

* Discriminator

The first discriminator now has a recursive first-class transformer,
independent finite-model equality, and paired join-probe/fix-iteration
counts over chain and star families. Its zero-change propagation
avoids old joins during a recursive frontier step. The finite interpreter
still rebuilds the old fixed point for each call. The
[[file:e4-retained-session.org][native Session gate]] instead retains the lowered rule state across
calls and checks append/withdrawal semantics. A matched larger-scale
and lifecycle-cost test remains open.
A transform that skips a delta without an equivalence argument is not
admitted.
