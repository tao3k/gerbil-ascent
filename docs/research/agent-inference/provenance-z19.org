* Primary method

[[https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05045][Provenance for Large-scale Datalog]], sections 2--3, PDF pages 7--14:
proof height annotations participate in evaluation, can improve after a tuple
first appears, and constrain a later proof-fragment search. Theorem 1 connects
the annotations to minimum-height trees. A depth-limited fragment and the
complete derivation collection have different meanings.

* Repository design decision

=candidate/provenance.ss= owns a replayable witness;
=candidate/provenance-graph.ss= owns complete bounded grounded alternatives;
=candidate/annotations.ss= owns all-tree unit natural counts. None currently
certifies minimum proof height. The first witness is not necessarily minimal;
productive recursion can bound natural counts while a finite-height witness
still exists.

A future minimum-height layer needs separate statuses for complete heights,
truncated fragments and failed computation. Its discriminator must discover
a long witness first, then a shorter alternative without adding a new output
tuple, and require the annotation to improve. Withdrawal must invalidate an
annotation whose chosen support disappears, retaining another founded support
when present. Lean owns minimum-height correctness; the existing snapshot and
publication models plus height-specific faults own protocol admission.
