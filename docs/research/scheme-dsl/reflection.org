* Claim discipline
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scheme-dsl-reflection
:END:

The proposed [[file:../../scheme-relational-language-design.org][Gerbil Scheme
language]] is a new design, not a claim to have surpassed its sources.
For each decision, separate what the authors established, what we
learned, our departure for Gerbil, and evidence that could reject it.
The [[file:index.org][source ledger]] preserves the primary PDFs and
individual readings.  Results from those implementations do not
transfer numerically to ours.

* Source-by-source reflection
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scheme-dsl-source-reflection
:END:

#+NAME: scheme-dsl-reflection-ledger
| Source | Established in the source | Lesson and our proposal | Limit of transfer |
|--------+---------------------------+-------------------------+-------------------|
| [[https://thomas.gilray.org/pdf/seamless-deductive.pdf][Ascent rule planning]], [[file:rule-planning.org][note]] | Rust macro embedding with relational planning, indexes, semi-naive evaluation and lattice examples; selected workload measurements | Retain inspectable rules, dependencies and delta planning while designing Scheme syntax independently | We have not matched its scale, diagnostics, or expressivity; Rust spelling is no design baseline |
| [[https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~nk480/datafun.pdf][Datafun functional fixed points]], [[file:functional-fixed-points.org][note]] | A typed calculus distinguishes discrete and monotone dependencies and reasons about fixed points over finite-height structure | Distinguish fixed parameters from changing relations; require a termination argument separate from monotonicity | A declared Gerbil descriptor has no Datafun typing proof |
| [[https://www.rntz.net/files/seminaive-datafun.pdf][Datafun higher-order change]], [[file:higher-order-change.org][note]] | A typed higher-order change translation, proof and evaluation | Specify an inspectable relation operator IR and reference change semantics before optimizing | Our finite structural insertion interpreter is narrower and has no transferred type or correctness proof |
| [[https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16684][DBSP incremental view maintenance]], [[file:incremental-view-maintenance.org][note]] | Stream-based incremental view maintenance, including recursive programs | Compare a retained native rule Session with fresh materialization, with explicit startup and memory costs | We implement no DBSP stream circuit or general signed update transformation |
| [[https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~olhotak/pubs/oopsla20c.pdf][Flix fragment composition]], [[file:fragment-composition.org][note]] | First-class constraint values and row-polymorphic composition checks, including stratification | Construct first-class fragments and check the assembled program | Runtime admission is weaker than Flix's static assurance over possible compositions |
| [[https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~olhotak/pubs/oopsla25b.pdf][Flix program lifecycle]], [[file:program-lifecycle.org][note]] | Program values, private predicates, inject/query, functional predicates and effects in the implemented language | Give each fragment invocation fresh private handles and separate lifecycle stages | We have not matched its type/effect guarantees, examples or ergonomics |
| [[https://www.schemeworkshop.org/2013/papers/HemannMuKanren2013.pdf][µKanren relational kernel]], [[file:relational-kernel.org][note]] | A small Scheme relational kernel with user-level forms and interleaved stream search | Keep a small semantic kernel beneath Scheme abstractions | Stream fairness, answer order and partial answers differ from bottom-up completion |

These papers address different semantics and workloads.  An aggregate
“better DSL” ranking would conceal those differences.  When a common
task cannot be specified, state a trade-off instead of forcing an A/B.

* Our departures and their costs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scheme-dsl-departures
:END:

#+NAME: scheme-dsl-departure-ledger
| Departure | Hypothesized benefit | Cost or counterargument | Required evidence |
|-----------+----------------------+-------------------------+-------------------|
| Scheme =(rule ...)= with =?variables= | Explicit logic-variable scope within Gerbil composition | New macro may have poor error locations or more verbose authoring | Native macro prototype, invalid-scope cases and paired authoring tasks |
| Fragment functions with fresh private handles | Parameterized reuse without accidental relation capture | Flix already supports first-class constraints and private names with stronger checks | Two-instance composition, signature conflict and late negative-cycle tests |
| Checkable operator IR with fixed/changing contexts | Planner can see dependencies and admission can reject hidden reads | Small IR may exclude useful Scheme and cost more to evaluate | Written semantics, sound admission argument, independent finite model and workload coverage |
| Frozen finite view table (implemented); general callback (proposed) | Visible modes and frozen finite input/output mapping | General callbacks still require purity, stability, effects and termination obligations | Frozen-capture/replay tests; infinite-successor and domain-failure discriminators |
| Separate =compose=, =admit=, =solve= and =query= | Program identity and completion become observable | Extra stages may burden users | Snapshot/source-replacement tests and paired API usability tasks |

“Fits Scheme better” and “is easier to use” remain hypotheses.  A
shorter example does not establish usability.  Borrowing an algorithmic
idea does not improve upon its original proof or implementation.

* Evidence ladder
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scheme-dsl-evidence
:END:

#+NAME: scheme-dsl-evidence-ladder
| Claim | Minimum evidence | Cannot establish alone |
|-------+------------------+------------------------|
| Semantic correctness | Written meaning, independent finite model, generated cases and counterexamples | General termination or host-function purity |
| Static assurance | A sound admission rule over a stated IR | Datafun/Flix-level type safety without their systems |
| Performance | Same semantics, pinned versions, matched inputs, operation counts and time/memory distributions | General speed across other workloads or hardware |
| Ergonomics | Paired tasks, comparable training, counterbalanced order, scored correctness/time/errors | Runtime performance or formal assurance |
| LLM usefulness | Fixed model, actual Scheme source/tasks, invocation and budget; no preset semantic guidance; score raw acceptance, semantic correctness and actual feedback separately | Changes to model weights or general reasoning ability |

Before a comparison, register the claim, baseline, treatment, common
semantic subset, primary outcome, task split, sample count, exclusion
rule and failure threshold.  Preserve exact commits, tool versions,
hardware, source data, commands, seeds, raw output and analysis script.
Report uncertainty, individual failures and adverse results.  Use
paired inputs where possible; counterbalance order for human tasks.

:END:

#+NAME: scheme-dsl-preregistered-comparisons
| ID | Baseline A | Proposal B | Primary outcome | Interpretation limit |
|----+------------+------------+-----------------+----------------------|
| E1 semantics | Independent finite-set/lattice model | Scheme DSL reference evaluator | Exact complete fact sets and explicit rejection/failure | Conformance, not paper superiority |
| E2 authoring | Current Scheme surface on a common positive-rule subset | Proposed native Scheme grammar | Correct solutions and located mistakes per fixed task | Old surface is a local control, not a proxy for Flix users |
| E3 composition | Direct duplicated declarations in current Scheme | Fragment constructor with fresh handles | Capture/collision errors and complete outputs | Flix already offers typed composition |
| E4 delta | Naive evaluator of the same admitted operator IR | Transformed evaluator | Exact equivalence, then operation count and scale | No transfer of Datafun higher-order change's measured gain |
| E5 engine | Current Scheme evaluator on a common logical subset | New Scheme evaluator | Time/memory distributions after exact-output check | No Rust/Flix ranking without matched runnable tasks |
| E6 later LLM | Same model and evidence without Library result | Same model/evidence with checked Library result | Answer correctness and unsupported claims | Outside the first DSL design gate |

The current local implementation in E2/E5 is an experimental control,
not a compatibility requirement.  If result semantics differ, restrict
the comparison to an explicit common subset or report two separate
outcomes.  Direct Ascent, Flix or Datafun comparisons require runnable
pinned implementations and matched tasks.  Otherwise report only our
measurements and the published context.  Preserve inconclusive and
negative results and revise the hypothesis.

The [[file:../../scheme-relational-language-design.org::#scheme-language-first-code][native code gate]]
exercises E1 across 64 finite reachability graphs: independent graph
closure, a separate operator reference interpreter, direct rule
lowering, composed operator fragments and the inert candidate path
produce equal complete sets. Another 64 growth cases compare reference
change and structural delta propagation with the independent
before/after set difference. The combined
fixture includes recursion, finite view, negation, grouped count, max
lattice and source withdrawal. E1 remains open for generated programs
beyond this grammar, finite lattice law evidence and larger domains.
E4 now has a [[file:e4-positive-change.org][bounded paired local receipt]]: exact sets match
an independent model; join probes and fixed-point body evaluations
favor structural change on chain and star cases. One small chain case
has worse median wall time, and larger timings contain outliers. A
general or asymptotic performance result remains open. E2, E3, E5 and
E6 have no measured A/B result.

The [[file:e4-retained-session.org][retained Session receipt]] adds an operational comparison within
E4: the same transformer is admitted once and receives a checked
source insertion, versus a fresh materialization on the grown source.
All 64 finite three-node graphs match an independent closure through
insertion, duplicate insertion and withdrawal. The single-update
probe has one adverse chain-12 median and noisy individual samples.
A separate chain-24 lifecycle case includes startup and eleven updates;
its two run medians favor retained, with overlapping samples. This is evidence for
this implementation and workload, not a typed change theorem or a
general optimizer ranking.

* Decision gate
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scheme-dsl-reflection-gate
:END:

Each proposed construct needs a source lesson, admitted IR node,
failure mode and independent test before implementation.  Build the
smallest native grammar and reference evaluator before tuning.  Add
optimized evaluation only after E1.  Do not call this DSL superior to a
paper system while a semantically matched comparison is absent.
