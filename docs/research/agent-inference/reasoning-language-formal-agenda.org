* Inspected source and design question

This note reads gerbil-ascent PR #1 at =8a2ecab= and the separate,
clean meta-relational-reasoning =main= checkout at =faedb3e=. The latter
uses Rust Ascent 0.8.0 for its bounded candidate kernel; it does not
currently call the Gerbil implementation. These are source snapshots,
not a claim that the two runtimes or proof projects are integrated.

The question is which *reasoning language* can let a model propose a
finite relational computation, execute it under a declared meaning,
and return evidence that MRR may evaluate for admission. The answer
must preserve three different boundaries:

1. Gerbil ASCENT defines and executes a checked relational program over
   a fixed input snapshot. Its program/fragment constructors and
   =relational-admit/solve/query= stages are visible in
   [[file:../../../program/scheme-language.ss][scheme-language]], [[file:../../../program/scheme-admission.ss][admission]],
   [[file:../../../program/scheme-query.ss][query]] and [[file:../../../candidate/reasoning.ss][candidate reasoning]].
2. An LLM's text or structured proposal is *candidate input*. A parser,
   valid typed program and completed execution are three different
   observations; none proves the proposal matches the user's intent.
3. MRR owns fact/relation identities, semantic generations, lineage,
   transition validation and publication. Its =crates/mrr-ascent/src/api.rs=
   produces bounded Rust-Ascent candidates without allocating those
   identities; =proofs/MRRProof/BundleAdmission.lean= proves selected
   bundle/identity admission properties under stated premises.

Thus the useful language is a typed, inspectable derivation kernel plus
an exact attempt/result envelope. It is not a general agent planning
language or a new authority for source facts.

* Language kernel and observations

The checked source already distinguishes declared source/relation/lattice
handles, rule bodies, negative clauses, fixed reducers, typed operators
and explicit limits. Keep these as *inspectable* objects, with a finite
scalar/row domain where a complete result is claimed. Program values
may compose, but dependency, binding, stratification and private-name
checks must run on the assembled program. A model may submit the inert
candidate representation only through the same admission boundary.

For a fixed admitted program =P= and frozen source =D_g=, the positive
kernel's intended meaning is =lfp(T[P,D_g])=. The observable outcome is
not just a row set; it also includes =complete|bounded|unsupported|rejected=,
the exact program/snapshot identity and a separately checked witness or
finite-ground nonmembership result. A completed *program* result still
does not authenticate =D_g= or establish that a missing KG edge is
false in the world.

The immediate design rule is to keep three equalities separate:

- tuple equality inside one admitted finite relation;
- private descriptor/predicate identity during composition and lowering;
- MRR's caller-owned =FactId=, =DerivationId= and =GenerationId= during
  admission and publication.

Conflating these makes a structurally valid model proposal look like
authoritative evidence. The current [[file:../../scheme-research-proof-audit.org::#cross-layer-refinement][refinement audit]] identifies the native
identity and representation obligations in more detail.

* Lean: proved kernels and next theorem

The [[file:../../scheme-actor-runtime-design.org][actor runtime decision]] now requires source-bound work keys, frozen
rounds, merge barriers, cancellation draining and unchanged completed publication.
=RoundAdmission= checks stale/duplicate/canceled reply rejection over unbounded
natural generations, including re-enabling acceptance after cancellation.
=ActorRound= is a finite protocol model with early-barrier, stale-reply and
canceled-publication fault controls. These are scheduling specifications;
native worker correspondence, termination and measured benefit remain open.
=AtomicBinding= connects ordinary positive atom substitution and known-column
keys to indexed clause equivalence under =BucketRep=. The separate native
assignment model keeps this proof premise falsifiable before actor parallelism.

The tracked =packages/proofs/lean/= modules currently include
=LexicalLowering= (typed lexical preservation), =RecursiveLowering=
(positive scoped feedback), =FiniteOperators= and =FiniteHeight=
(monotone/finite-rank laws), =DescriptorScope=, =CappedArithmetic=,
=PositiveNonmembership=, =SessionPublication=, =NativeSessionPublication=
and =StratifiedNegation=. The
[[file:../../scheme-compiler-proof-bridge.org][proof bridge]] states their hypotheses and concrete omissions. These
proofs are valuable but do not yet relate native Scheme scalars,
descriptor graphs, emitted rules or table steps to the Lean objects.

| Existing statement | Established abstract claim | Still missing for a language claim |
|--------------------+----------------------------+------------------------------------|
| =LexicalLowering.lowering_preserves_meaning= | Typed expressions and their relational IR have related denotations | Actual admitted Scheme descriptors translate to that source calculus |
| =RecursiveLowering.lowered_feedback_is_least= | Scoped positive feedback preserves the least relation | Emitted rules, SCC/delta plans and concrete table steps refine the IR |
| =FiniteHeight.bounded_bottom_least_fixed= | An ascending finite-rank orbit reaches the least fixed point under its premises | Native type/domain interpretation supplies the rank and every native step preserves its order |
| =PositiveNonmembership.ground_why_not_sound= | Ground absence follows from the model's closure premises | Native certificate checking and caller source-completeness are separate |
| =SessionPublication.publication_consistent= and =publications_consistent= | One or any finite sequence of qualified publications retains source/result consistency over unbounded natural-number generations | Native Session steps implement the abstraction and qualification is independently established |
| =NativeSessionPublication.reachable_good= | For arbitrary immutable cuts and natural-number generations, every finite sequence of the six synchronous normal actions preserves committed result consistency, clean alignment and bounded status | Gerbil entrypoints must refine those actions; complete solves must be qualified, and failed recovery, source appends and mutable host values are outside the model |
| =StratifiedNegation.visit_iff= and =evaluateBranches_iff= | A frozen-stratum atom/negation clause walk has the stated branch-local set meaning; a direct witness survives a blocker on the via branch | =gerbil-ascent-bind-row= and =indexed-rows= must refine the modeled match and index-exactness premises; no recursive stratum or aggregation is covered |

The new clause proof follows the control path in =program/evaluate.ss=:
positive atoms extend the environment; negation checks for a matching
row without adding bindings. =IndexExact= makes the index requirement
explicit: candidates come from the full frozen relation and retain
every row that matches the current binding. Under that premise,
=atom_indexed_iff= and =negation_indexed_iff= preserve the clause result.
For a logical key filter, =keyed_rows_exact= reduces that premise to
one condition: every row accepted by the binder has the key computed
from the current environment. =keyed_negation_iff= then preserves the
negative clause. Actual hash buckets, custom providers and the plan's
key selection still need representation proofs against this filter.
The next slice makes the canonical bucket algorithm explicit:
=build_bucket_rep= and =extend_bucket_rep= prove that prepend-based
build/extension retain exactly the full relation's matching members
at every key. =ground_bucket_negation_iff= composes that invariant with
the callback-free admitted negation subset (literal, previously bound
variable, wildcard): successful row binding entails the same ordered
key as the index lookup. This is set-membership semantics, so duplicate
source occurrences and bucket order remain separate observations.
The actual =hash-get=/=hash-put!= implementation, column extraction,
scalar equality, cache versioning and custom provider contracts are
still native interpretation obligations. The existing
=t/qualification/ascent-index-lifecycle-test.ss= compares physical
buckets with a frozen reference across build, extension, arbitrary
columns and retained Session updates; it is finite native evidence,
not a proof of these obligations for all admitted programs.
The =frozen_blocker_step_monotone= theorem states the corresponding
fixed-point premise: with a fixed lower-stratum blocker, growth of the
positive current-stratum input cannot retract branch results if its
positive via operator is monotone.
The proof does not establish that Scheme's index planner satisfies this
premise, or that the checked rule planner always produces the modeled
clause sequence. Those are the next implementation refinement obligations.

The next high-value theorem is *native-step refinement for one finite
positive fragment*, rather than another abstract least-fixed-point
lemma. Define explicit =TypeRep=, =ValueRep= and =StepRep= relations for
admitted immutable scalars/rows and one source, join, selection,
projection and recursive rule. Then prove:

#+begin_example
admitted(P, D_g) and StepRep(native_state, abstract_state)
and one successful native step
  => StepRep(native_state', T[P,D_g](abstract_state))
completed(native_state) and the finite-height premise
  => ResultRep(native_rows, lfp(T[P,D_g]))
#+end_example

The actual compiled plan may perform several index/delta microsteps
per semantic step, so the first relation may need a stuttering
simulation rather than a one-to-one equality. Duplicate input
occurrences, zero-arity tuples, scalar hash/equality, private-name
renaming and escaped captures are mandatory discriminators. =bounded=
and =unsupported= cannot satisfy the completed conclusion. For a
future mono type, prove its *lowering correspondence* from reachable
container observations to relation rows; merely repeating the Rocq
=add/read= laws in Lean would miss the native implementation.

MRR's =BundleAdmission.lean= proves identity uniqueness, status and
owner-premise rejection, and typed portions of its query admission.
It does not prove that Rust Ascent closure or Gerbil ASCENT results are
semantically correct, nor that a caller supplied true source facts.
A future cross-repository theorem would compose a complete,
generation-bound candidate result with MRR's accepted identity,
lineage and transition premises. The two proof projects should not be
silently identified by matching type names.

* TLA+: present lifecycle models and missing bridge

[[file:../../../packages/proofs/tla/SessionTransaction.tla][SessionTransaction]] models atomic source/result publication, stale
work and abort over one symbolic direct/via/blocked row. Its =ResultOf=
is the branch-local formula from the Lean slice; a =global= mutation
puts exclusion after union and must violate =AtomicSnapshot= when a
blocked direct witness is published. This compares the same formula
across the two artifacts but is not a mechanized Lean-to-TLA translation.
A =reuse= mutation keeps the old answer when only the blocker bit
changes; it must also violate =AtomicSnapshot= once a via witness is
retracted or restored. This directly checks that a lower-stratum
source change is not treated as monotone growth of the old answer.
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/tla/PositiveNonmembershipSession.tla][PositiveNonmembershipSession]] models certificate binding
and stale-use rejection. Their =Cuts= are tiny symbolic domains for
TLC; neither model is a native Gerbil trace refinement, source
authentication model or liveness proof. The inspected MRR checkout at
=faedb3e= has one tracked Lean file and no tracked =.tla= or =.cfg=
file; MRR's README describes TLA evidence at a broader architecture
level, so that claim should be reconciled before asserting a passing
MRR-side TLC gate.

The actual checked invariants here are =AtomicSnapshot= in the
transaction model and =NoStalePublished/NoStaleUse= in the certificate
model. They speak about protocol state and the symbolic branch result,
not a native least-fixed-point computation.

The [[file:../../../packages/proofs/tla/NativeSessionPublication.tla][NativeSessionPublication]] slice follows the *synchronous* retained
Session entrypoints more closely. =committed=, =pending= and =published=
abstract =program/session.ss='s accepted committed source, pending source
and =committed-result=, respectively. =published= deliberately does not
map to =last-result=: =run-timeout!= may return a receipt whose =finished=
flag is false while retaining the previous completed result. The model
distinguishes atomic completed batches, provisional single-source
replacement, completed runs, bounded runs, and failure restoration.
=CommittedSnapshot=, =CleanAligned=, =PartialBounded= and
=NoPrematurePublication= are checked over its finite cuts; =early= and
=bounded= mutations must expose premature publication. Its logical
=generation= counts completed commits, but there is no corresponding
native public generation token. Source appends, callback failures inside
adoption, opaque providers and native trace refinement remain outside
this model.

The matching [[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/NativeSessionPublication.lean][Lean transition model]] proves =reachable_good= by induction for
arbitrary cuts and any finite trace. It also proves that provisional,
bounded, rejected and restored-failure actions leave the committed
public triple unchanged. This proves the transition model, not a
mechanically extracted Gerbil implementation or a Lean/TLA syntax
equivalence. The native correspondence is currently a review obligation:

| Native entrypoint / path | Abstract action | Unproved condition |
|--------------------------+-----------------+--------------------|
| =replace-source!= after completed preflight | =SingleReplace= | Internal batch work is unobservable before the synchronous method returns; committed result and source are restored together |
| =append-source!= with an owned immutable row | =SingleReplace= at the public cut | Native incremental engine state and its eventual completed solve still require refinement |
| =replace-sources!= completed | =BatchComplete= | =fresh.run= is complete and its rows equal a fresh solve for the prospective admitted snapshot |
| =replace-sources!= rejected | =BatchReject= | No accepted source/result pair changes on validation or solve failure |
| =run!= or completed =run-timeout!= | =RunComplete= | The engine result is complete and matches the pending source snapshot |
| bounded =run-timeout!= | =RunBounded= | =committed-result= stays fixed, even though the returned =last-result= is partial |
| failed run with successful restore | =RunFailure= | Rebuilding from =committed= succeeds; a second restoration failure needs a separate fail-closed state |

An added =alias= fault changes a committed source cut without changing
its published result; TLC and Lean both reject it. This is a concrete
counterexample to extending the proof to arbitrary mutable host values:
=program/session.ss= previously retained caller-owned row pairs during
batch replacement and append. It now copies replacement list/row spines
after validation and captures each appended row spine before handing it
to the engine. The native regressions mutate caller-owned rows after
both operations, then compare completed output with fresh solves. The
checked language admits only exact integers, booleans,
symbols and characters as scalar fields, and copies constructed rows.
Nested host objects remain shared; opaque callbacks/providers require
an ownership or copying contract before their completed results inherit
this proof. A passing native regression qualifies this ownership boundary;
a full native trace refinement is still open.

The proof package now uses LeanPOO directly through Lake. The
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/SourceCertificates.lean][source-certificate module]] represents each source cut as a typed
=LeanPoo.Proof.State= and an ordered replacement as =Patch.setMany=.
A derived-result =Obligation= declares its source dependencies and a
locality premise. LeanPOO's =reuse= transports a certificate across a
disjoint batch; =pending= selects affected and new obligations;
=closePending= requires fresh evidence for those selected. The module
composes a batch patch with =NativePublication.batchComplete= and proves
the published result belongs to the new cut while untouched source
keys retain their values. Concrete qualification cases live in
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/AscentProofTests.lean][AscentProofTests]]. These statements require an accurate dependency
list and completed =resultOf=; native source-dependency closure, typed
row representation and evaluator correspondence remain open. The same
module makes =StratifiedNegation.IndexExact= a LeanPOO obligation over a
frozen full relation and its candidate bucket. A current certificate
discharges =negation_indexed_iff=; changing the source relation selects
that certificate for revalidation. Native hash-bucket fidelity remains
an independent premise.

The source-update result is now one compositional contract rather than a
collection of unrelated graph lemmas. For each admitted relation, the old
source cut is =base ++ reverse(newest-first appends)=; the prospective rows
form the new cut. Changed source IDs seed a directed graph in which every
positive, negative and aggregate body read points to every rule head.
The selected set is the least reachability closure of those seeds. This
includes a recursive path head, then negative and aggregate dependents,
while an independent relation remains outside the selected set.

The [[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/SourceUpdateFrame.lean][SourceUpdateFrame.completed_reuse theorem]] composes that source comparison
with the typed bitmap worklist, the read-checklist evaluator and the
completed fixed-point frame theorem. It proves that, for two runs which
may finish after different numbers of rounds, *every unselected relation*
has the same completed value. =affected_iff= proves exact membership in
the computed closure; =source_frame= proves the initial rows agree outside
it. The proof requires =readsMatch=: each evaluator read is either the
target's own previous value or a body-read edge in the compiled graph.
The self-read covers native deduplication and lattice join without adding
a redundant graph edge; =AscentProofTests= instantiates this case. The
proof also requires both results to be stable completed
fixed points. =FunctionalDependency.Program.local= discharges per-step
locality from LeanPOO's typed =Requirements= checklist; it does not infer
=readsMatch= from the native evaluator. The finite =count + 1= fuel proves
termination of graph traversal only, not of recursive evaluation.

The constituent proofs make the contract auditable. The
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/RuleGraphClosure.lean][rule compiler]] proves exact source-to-head membership, including repeated
heads and all three read kinds. The
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/NativeGraphWorklist.lean][list worklist]] proves mark-before-enqueue termination for dense relation
IDs and exact closure even with cycles and duplicate edges. The
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/NativeBitmapWorklist.lean][bitmap refinement]] relates each typed =Vector Bool= update to that list
machine. The
[[file:../../../packages/proofs/lean/DependencyInvalidation.lean][fixed-point frame]] carries equality outside the closed set through
arbitrary rounds and then through differently timed completed results.
=AscentProofTests= includes a concrete source-log/recursive/negative/
aggregate chain with an independent relation, plus the counterexample
where omitting a negative-read edge incorrectly retains a changed result.
Lake pins LeanPOO PR #8 at =9e160953=; only its typed Requirements
contract is used here.

Native evidence exercises the same public update behavior. The graph
qualification compares all 64 three-relation directed graphs and all
eight seed subsets with an independent matrix oracle, checks mixed
lowered read kinds, and traverses a 4,096-relation chain. The stratified
Session qualification now requires the actual dependency-invalidation
path: a changed blocker recomputes negative and aggregate dependents,
retains the completed recursive path, and agrees with a fresh solve on
*all* result relations; a subsequent combined blocker/weight replacement
checks the same property. These are execution checks, not a proof that
Gerbil's mutable vectors and evaluator refine the Lean model. The open
bridge is concrete: admitted IDs must be in range, source row ownership
must preserve the compared cut, and each native rule's actual reads must
be included in =readsMatch=. A custom index Provider can capture mutable
state beyond that graph. Reuse admission now accepts only the private
canonical hash Provider, and a native regression uses a 32-row indexed
join to show that changing an opaque lookup forces fresh evaluation.
Opaque rule callbacks and custom storage Providers also use full
evaluation. Field-checker callbacks remain an explicit native refinement
obligation: their effects are exercised by qualification but are not
proved by this pure read-checklist model. No performance claim follows
from this proof.

The =TLCGenerationCutoff= constant is a finite *model-checking* search
parameter, supplied by =ASCENT_TLC_GENERATION_CUTOFF= in the =Justfile= (four
generations in CI). Direct =.cfg= invocation samples two generations.
Neither value limits a native Session, an ASCENT fixed point, or a model's
reasoning budget. Lean's =publications_consistent= proves the abstract
publication invariant for a list of any length; that theorem still assumes
each completed result is qualified and does not establish native refinement.

The next cross-owner safety model belongs at the ASCENT-to-MRR
publication boundary. Model =Propose=, =Solve=, =SourceChange=,
=CheckIdentity=, =CheckLineage=, =CheckTransition= and =Publish= as
separate actions. At a minimum, require these invariants:

#+begin_example
Published => Complete and SameProgram and SameGeneration
             and IdentityAccepted and LineageAccepted and TransitionAccepted
RejectedOrBounded => PublishedStateUnchanged
SourceChange => OldReceiptCannotPublish
#+end_example

The negative controls are a stale solve after a source change, an ABA
source payload with a new generation, a valid-looking candidate with
one rejected lineage edge, and a complete relation with a failed
transition check. TLC can find protocol counterexamples on finite
symbols; it cannot prove the Scheme evaluator, Rust adapter, cryptographic
binding or real-world source completeness. Safety is the first target;
fairness/liveness is separate because a bounded solve may legitimately
fail or never produce a complete result.

* First falsifiable study

The [[file:wikidata-scope-wd26.org][WD26 fixture]] starts this study with twelve selected claims from
seven Wikidata entity revisions, a pinned target entity, a gold structured program and
two local source generations. Its independently computed finite set
answer discriminates branch-local exclusion from exclusion after union.
This is a small executor test, not a model benchmark or a transactional
Wikidata snapshot.

Submit an
unaltered model proposal once; retain its raw response, then score
admission, complete relation rows, branch-scoped negation, witness and
final answer against independent finite data. Remove a direct edge but
preserve an alternative *admitted* rule path; also include a truly
unsupported edge and an incomplete-source status. This separates
deduction from lookup and global absence from scoped nonmembership.
BRINK's [[https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.114/][incomplete-KG construction]] suggests the counterfactual, but its
AMIE3-mined rules are empirical correlations and cannot be the semantic
oracle for this test.

If that case passes, repeat on a real-code relation task, and measure
typed program correctness, source/witness fidelity, intermediate work
and end-answer quality separately. No change to model weights, MRR
publication authority or general deployed benefit follows from a
single fixture. The [[file:../../scheme-design-framework.org][design framework]] keeps paid model comparison and Rust
integration as later, independent gates.
