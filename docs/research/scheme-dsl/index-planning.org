#+TITLE: Souffle synthesis and deterministic native index planning
#+DATE: 2026-10-09

* Primary method reading

[[https://souffle-lang.github.io/pdf/cav16.pdf][Jordan, Scholz and Subotic, CAV 2016]] separates Datalog-to-RAM,
RAM-to-C++ and C++ compilation specialization. Section 2.1 locates join/index
planning before fact execution; its chain partition minimizes index count.
This is a program-specific planning architecture, not just a faster interpreter.

The later [[https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol12/p141-subotic.pdf][Subotic et al., PVLDB 12(2), 2018]] supplies the detailed index
selection method. Equality-bound attribute sets are primitive searches. A
physical key covers a search when those attributes form its prefix. Strict
subset chains share one index; minimum chain partition reduces to maximum
bipartite matching. The theorem concerns coverage/count, not a uniquely chosen
permutation, and the clustered B-tree performance results depend on the studied
programs and representation.

* Repository architecture and implemented gap

=table/index-sharing.ss= already implements chain matching, an independent tight
endpoint-cover certificate and curried physical roots. =IndexedPositiveTraversal=
already proves competing partition minimality; =CurriedDictionary= handles
native prefix occurrence restoration under its inventory premise. These are
existing accepted mechanisms, not new work inferred from the paper.

The remaining gap addressed here is reproducibility of equally minimal plans.
Normalization formerly fed hash-key encounter order into matching. The native
planner now lexicographically orders detached, sorted, distinct nonempty column
sets. Every augmentation and chain publication consumes that fixed order.
Empty demand sets still require no physical index; singleton representation,
source occurrence order, independent owners and maximum-matching checks remain
covered by the existing native suite.

* Executable refinement and limits

All 128 three-column demand families retain independent minimum partition and
prefix coverage checks. Three additional encounter/column/multiplicity variants
per family compare physical layouts. Existing large exact integer, detached
input, solve parity and trie occurrence tests remain in the same native Suite.

Lean =CanonicalIndex= proves inventory membership, demand-set invariance,
encounter-order invariance, duplicate invariance and deterministic planner
identity. Its fixed inventory abstraction does not extract Scheme sorting or
prove arbitrary native mutation safe. Quint =CanonicalIndex= explores finite
demand collection/publication and requires early publication, omission,
duplicate and reversed-order controls to violate designated invariants.

This does not implement Souffle RAM/C++ synthesis, inequalities, clustered
B-trees, DOOP/OpenJDK replication or a measured solve speedup. The historical
=column-order= branching exact-layout rejection remains recorded; its arbitrary
baseline tie choices are not a requirement for the new canonical policy.
Existing performance thresholds and CI deadlines are unchanged.
